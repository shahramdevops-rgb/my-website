## What carried over

The tools changed; the way of thinking didn't. Three ideas I use every week came straight from engineering school.

<Bridges>
    <Bridge from="Control systems & feedback" to="Observability">
        A controller is only as good as its sensors: you can't regulate what you don't measure. A
        running system is the same — logs, metrics and traces are the sensors that close the loop
        between what I deploy and what people actually get.
    </Bridge>
    <Bridge from="State machines" to="Domain invariants">
        A digital circuit is only ever in a defined state, and only some transitions are legal. I
        model business entities the same way: a booking goes from reserved to paid to used, and the
        code makes every other move impossible.
    </Bridge>
    <Bridge from="Designing within tolerances" to="Systems under hard constraints">
        Real components drift, heat up and fail, so a good circuit works across its whole tolerance
        range. I design software the same way: for slow networks, users acting at the same moment
        and partial failures — not only the happy path.
    </Bridge>
</Bridges>
